LLM-Based AI Agents

Foundations, Methods, and Applications

From reasoning and tool use to trustworthy, collaborative systems. A connected introduction to the lifecycle of agentic AI.

Explore the programMeet the speakers

Overview

LLM-based AI agents extend language models into goal-directed systems that reason, plan, maintain memory, retrieve information, use tools, and interact with humans and other agents. Understanding these systems means connecting their individual capabilities to the design, evaluation, and responsible deployment of a complete agentic system.

This tutorial follows that lifecycle through six connected modules: foundations; architectures, methods, and implementation; evaluation and human oversight; multi-agent systems; applications and scientific discovery; and emerging research directions. A running data-intelligence case connects the modules, following an agent as it retrieves evidence, invokes analytical tools, maintains context, and produces an evidence-grounded result under human oversight.

Drawing on the structure of the forthcoming book LLM-Based AI Agents: Foundations, Methods, and Applications, the tutorial emphasizes transferable concepts across AI, machine learning, planning, data management, and human–AI interaction.

What you will learn

  • Distinguish LLM calls, workflows, individual agents, and multi-agent systems.
  • Connect reasoning, planning, memory, retrieval, and tool use within an agent loop.
  • Evaluate outcomes, trajectories, and individual actions, including their risks and costs.
  • Design appropriate oversight and identify when multi-agent collaboration adds value.

Who should attend

Researchers, graduate students, AI developers, data scientists, practitioners, and educators interested in agentic AI.

Basic familiarity with machine learning and LLMs is helpful. No experience with a particular agent framework is required.

Short activities use prepared diagrams and execution traces. No installation, API access, or special computing resources are needed.

Agenda

Six modules follow an agent from its first goal to evaluation, collaboration, and real-world applications.

  1. 15 minModule I

    Foundations of LLM-Based AI Agents

    From conversational LLMs to goal-directed systems. The observe–reason–plan–act lifecycle, core agent components, autonomy, environmental feedback, and the choice between agents and fixed workflows.

    Wenbin Zhang
  2. 30 minModule II

    Architectures, Methods, and Implementation

    Reasoning and planning, short- and long-term memory, retrieval, context engineering, tool use, orchestration, observability, and approaches to agent training and improvement.

    Jie (Jack) Yang
  3. 25 minModule III

    Evaluation, Trustworthiness, and Human Oversight

    Outcome-, trajectory-, and action-level evaluation. Safety, reliability, security, privacy, fairness, explainability, runtime guardrails, and meaningful human intervention.

    Wenbin Zhang
  4. 15 minModule IV

    Multi-Agent Systems and Agentic Ecosystems

    Communication, coordination, role specialization, shared state, and orchestration. When to use multiple agents, and how to reason about coordination costs and failure propagation.

    Jie (Jack) Yang
  5. 10 minModule V

    Applications and Autonomous Scientific Discovery

    Agents for data analysis, software engineering, web interaction, healthcare, and enterprise systems. Scientific workflows connect literature search, hypotheses, experimentation, validation, and reproducibility.

    Zhoutian Han
  6. 10 minModule VI

    Emerging Directions and Open Problems

    Continual learning, persistent agents, personalization, multimodal and embodied systems, scalable oversight, and the open challenges of increasingly autonomous agent ecosystems.

    Jie (Jack) Yang

Proposed program · 105 minutes in total · Session date, time, and room to be announced.

Wenbin Zhang

Assistant Professor
Florida International University

Wenbin Zhang is an Assistant Professor in the Knight Foundation School of Computing and Information Sciences at Florida International University and an Associate Member of the Te Ipu o Te Mahara Artificial Intelligence Institute. His research focuses on the foundations of machine learning, responsible and socially beneficial AI, agentic AI, AI alignment, and AI for science. He teaches graduate AI and responsible-AI courses and has presented tutorials on fairness in language models, generative-AI copyright, and LLM applications at international venues including IJCAI, CIKM, and ICDM. He has received an NSF CRII Award and research distinctions at FAccT, ICDM, and ECML PKDD, and contributes to international AI conferences through organizing and senior program-committee roles.

Jie (Jack) Yang

Senior Lecturer
University of Wollongong

Dr. Jie (Jack) Yang is a Senior Lecturer in the School of Computing and Information Technology at the University of Wollongong (UOW), Australia. His research focuses on Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly trustworthy and responsible AI, large language models (LLMs), and multimodal learning and reasoning. He has published over 100 research papers in leading international journals and conferences and has contributed to several Australian Research Council (ARC) and industry-funded research projects. Beyond his research, he is actively involved in teaching, research supervision, academic leadership, and industry collaboration. He also serves as the Australia Program Director for UOW's onshore and offshore computing programs.

Song Fang is an Associate Professor in the Knight Foundation School of Computing and Information Sciences at Florida International University.
